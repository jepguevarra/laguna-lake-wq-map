    About the data

    The values come from the LLDA Quarterly Water Quality Report on Laguna Lake and Tributary Rivers, four reports covering July 2025 to June 2026. Each figure is one month's reading. For fecal coliform it is that month's geometric mean.

    Lake stations (16) measure the open water. River stations (37) sit on the tributaries that drain the surrounding towns into the lake. Where a river has an upstream (U) or midstream (M) station, the plain number is the downstream station near the river mouth.

    Pass/fail is judged against the DENR Class C criteria the reports use. Class C is the water quality Laguna de Bay is legally held to: fit for fisheries, boating and industrial supply. Several parameters have no Class C limit.

    Limitations

    • Station positions were fitted from the station map printed in each report, georeferenced against the OpenStreetMap lake boundary at about 43 m per pixel. That is far tighter than the ±1.5 km of the older data, but they are still read off a printed map, not surveyed.
    • The “Lake shoreline” overlay is the OpenStreetMap boundary of Laguna de Bay, thinned to about 10 m.
    • Some stations were not sampled every month. Station 20 (Jala-jala River) has no readings at all: it was replaced by Binangonan River in June 2022 but is still listed. Gaps are drawn as “not sampled” rather than guessed at.
    • A reading printed as “<0.1” was below what the laboratory could measure. The charts use that limit, which is its honest upper bound, and the station table shows the original.
    • The town listed for a river is where the station is. Rivers also carry pollution from towns upstream.
    • The values were read by eye from scanned reports, which have no machine-readable text. Each table was straightened first, and the gaps match what the reports say in words, but transcription error cannot be ruled out.
